Abstract

A large share of AI hardware value is priced on the assumption that frontier training must happen inside one building, and that assumption is a number, 0.8 bytes per second per parameter in the unsharded data-parallel case, past a 400 Gb/s port at 62.5B parameters at a 5 second step. Methods that need less bandwidth exist at published exchange rates, and the multi-site record over ordinary links moved from 1.1B to a completed 72B in under two years, with a partial 100B demonstration alongside, against a dense frontier of 540B. Base case: the premium holds. The scenario worth watching is low-communication training reaching frontier scale; the tail, the part I know from my own dissertation, is evolutionary methods closing their sample gap. Three pre-registered criteria state what would change the base case, a daily collector instruments the first.

The Bandwidth Arithmetic

In the pure data-parallel case modelled here, every worker holds a full replica, computes gradients on its shard of the batch, and the fleet averages those gradients before the next step. The averaging is an all-reduce, and a bandwidth-optimal ring all-reduce moves close to twice the size of the exchanged buffer through each worker, every step.1 1. Ring all-reduce moves 2(N-1)/N of the buffer per worker (Patarasuk and Yuan, 2009). The factor is taken as 2 throughout; source 14.

Worked once, at 70B parameters: gradients in bf16 are 2 bytes per parameter, a 140 GB buffer, the ring moves twice that through each worker, 280 GB per step, and frontier runs step every few seconds, the stated target here being 5.2 2. Derived from the Llama 3.1 405B disclosure: 6 FLOPs per parameter-token, a 16M token batch and 16,384 H100s at 400 TFLOPS each imply a 5.9 second step; source 9. That demands 56 GB/s of sustained interconnect per worker, more than a 400 Gb/s InfiniBand port delivers at line rate, so a 70B run modelled this way has already outgrown building-scale networking. The general form is required bandwidth = 0.8 bytes per second per parameter, and the illustrative crossings follow: consumer fibre by 160M parameters, campus ethernet by 15.6B, a 400 Gb/s port by 62.5B, and an NVLink-class link by 1.1T.3 3. A worker in Exhibit A is one data-parallel rank holding a full replica. Payload efficiency and protocol overhead are omitted. Sharding and overlap change per-device traffic; see the comparison below and source 9.

The bound is not a per-NIC estimate for a sharded frontier run. Meta's disclosed Llama 3 405B layout used tensor parallelism of 8 and pipeline parallelism of 16. Dividing the same gradient payload across those 128 GPUs gives approximately 2.1 GB/s of gradient-sync traffic per GPU at the disclosed 5.9 second step, before tensor-, pipeline- and context-parallel traffic or protocol overhead. Frontier runs fit inside buildings in part by buying this additional parallelism and fabric.

This is one physical basis of scale-up fabric, co-packaged optics and rack-scale system value; HBM addresses a separate local-memory bottleneck, though it is sold inside many of the same systems.

The Method Spectrum

Synchronous gradient training is the efficiency baseline: best use of every sample, full exchange every step, communication scaling with model size. Everything above the 100 Gb/s band in the exhibit exists to serve it, which is another way of saying the premium is priced off this one method.

Low-communication gradient methods run hundreds of optimiser steps locally and synchronise compressed pseudo-gradients at long intervals. DiLoCo demonstrated 500 local steps at a modest quality cost; the INTELLECT runs carried the recipe to real distance, training 10B parameters across three continents with roughly 400 times less communication than synchronous data parallelism.4 4. DiLoCo, source 1; Streaming DiLoCo adds overlap and 4-bit quantisation, source 2; INTELLECT-1 technical report, source 4. The exchange rate is explicit: orders of magnitude less bandwidth, a few points of efficiency, more total compute for equal loss. Fabric pricing power rests on that exchange rate: every order of magnitude conceded moves training capacity from inside the building to outside it.

Evolutionary and population-based methods hold the far end of the curve. Workers perturb parameters under shared random seeds, evaluate, and exchange scalar fitness values: a few bytes per worker per generation, independent of model size. The published price is sample inefficiency, 3 to 10 times the data of a gradient baseline, measured on reinforcement-learning tasks rather than language pretraining.5 5. Salimans et al., 2017, source 6: evolution strategies scaled to over a thousand workers with bytes-per-generation communication. Qiu et al., 2025, source 22; MeZO, source 23. That price is regime-dependent and it is moving: the first full-parameter evolution-strategies fine-tuning of billion-parameter language models, published September 2025, outperformed the reinforcement-learning methods it was compared against on reasoning tasks, needing only forward passes, with fewer reward hacks and steadier runs, and zeroth-order fine-tuning has separately reached 66B parameters on inference-class memory.

My dissertation used these methods, so the sample penalty is something I have measured rather than read about. The work ran on a single machine, nothing distributed about it, and against a tuned gradient baseline the penalty came out at 4 to 8 times, inside the published range. What makes the method matter here is a property I did not need and did not test: members of a population only have to share a fitness number, so scaling out costs bytes wherever the workers sit. My read on the published range: solid where it was measured, on reinforcement-learning benchmarks at the scales research budgets reach, but I suspect part of the gap is research attention rather than method. Gradient training has had a decade of optimiser and infrastructure engineering behind it, evolutionary methods have not, and nobody has funded the equivalent effort to find where the floor actually is. The September 2025 result above is the first funded attempt, and it points the direction I expected. One result, one regime, so still a suspicion rather than a conclusion.

The three families sit on one curve, and the gradient world has spent two years descending it, cutting its bandwidth requirement by two and a half orders of magnitude for a few points of efficiency. The premium prices the assumption that the descent stops here; the distance record below measures whether it does.

What The Market Says

Rental marketplaces put a public price on the same accelerator in different machines, and the spread between whole NVLink nodes and standalone cards is a daily proxy for what the fabric is worth, only a proxy, machine, host, region and bundle composition move the same number.

The Distance Record

The record that bears on the thesis is the largest model trained across sites over links anyone can buy, and it has moved quickly: OpenDiLoCo took 1.1B parameters across two continents in July 2024, INTELLECT-1 took 10B across three continents that November on links the arithmetic above says need only about 128 Mb/s per worker, and INTELLECT-2 ran 32B in 2025, though as reinforcement-learning post-training, which exchanges far less than pretraining.7 7. Sources 3, 4, 5. RL post-training moves weights and rollouts occasionally, not gradients every step; the 32B point is a weaker claim than the 10B one. In March 2026 Covenant-72B completed roughly 1.1T tokens of pretraining at 72B parameters with open permissionless participation, peers joining and leaving freely.8 8. Source 24. SparseLoCo communication-efficient optimiser; reported as competitive with centralised models on similar or higher compute budgets. In June 2026 a controlled deployment across five sites took a 100B dense model onto links with a median upload of 856 Mbps, at roughly 65 percent of the operator's co-located baseline, and stopped after 1.1B tokens for cost.9 9. Sources 20 and 21: 16 pipeline stages x 3 replicas, 48 A100s, 30.8 percent MFU, figures the operator's own. Feasibility at 100B, not a completed pretraining; permissionless is stated future work. The partial run touches criterion 2's scale threshold without meeting it, the completed one sits 28B below it, and neither carries the documented compute accounting the criterion requires.

Frontier laboratories also train across datacentres: Gemini was trained across multiple sites on private fibre. Owned, provisioned, exclusive links extend the radius of one building without changing its cost of entry, so they carry no evidence about commodity links.

The gap is the number to track. In mid 2024 the commodity-link record stood roughly 500 times below the largest disclosed dense run, PaLM at 540B on two TPU pods over Google's own network. By 2025 the gap was about 17 times, and by March 2026, on Covenant's completed run, about 7.5 times. The dense benchmark is conservative: disclosed mixture-of-experts training reached 1.04T total parameters on 15.5T tokens in 2025, and a mixture model's gradients cover its total parameters, not its active ones, so the distance to centralised practice is wider than the dense line shows.12 12. Kimi K2, source 25: 1.04T total, 32B active. Completed runs set the direction; criterion 2 states the crossing that would settle it. Each crossing on the way converts standalone fleets from stranded capacity into competing supply, and useful-life assumptions register it first.

The View

Base case, the premium holds, and while it holds value keeps accruing where the bandwidth requirement is met, which the filings already show: NVIDIA reported 142 percent growth in Data Center networking revenue in fiscal 2026 driven by NVLink fabric, Ethernet and InfiniBand, Broadcom attributes its AI semiconductor demand to custom accelerators and Ethernet switching, and Micron reported higher HBM demand from AI data centres.10 10. Sources 15 to 17. These disclosures establish demand for the components; they do not measure a standalone interconnect premium. The costs of the alternatives are real, and capacity behind weaker links cannot hold the 5 second step, which is the constraint the premium prices.

The live scenario is low-communication training reaching frontier scale, because that trajectory is funded and public: 500 local steps demonstrated in 2023, 10B across three continents in 2024, 32B in 2025, a completed 72B and a partial 100B in 2026. If it continues to criterion 2 the first effects are accounting effects, because standalone and prior-generation fleets re-enter the training pool and earn longer, which moves useful-life and depreciation assumptions inside hyperscaler accounts before it moves any headline,11 11. Useful-life estimates have moved both ways. Microsoft extended server and network equipment from four to six years in 2022. Amazon extended servers to six years in 2024, then shortened a subset to five and accelerated retirements from 2025, citing the pace of AI development. Sources 18 and 19. and only after that networking attach and the bundle premium of integrated racks, which shifts back toward merchant silicon as compute becomes fungible across sites. Papers lead demonstrated runs by quarters, accounting follows within a reporting cycle or two, the rental spread moves last because marketplaces price bundles. The filings and the published runs are known, the sequencing is my inference.

The tail is evolutionary parity, and the tail is where my weighting differs from consensus. Structurally these methods fit a permissionless network better than anything else on the curve, independent workers exchanging a scalar, free to join and drop, no orchestrator, while the Orion run forced coupled gradient training across sites with a coordinator and 64x activation compression, the friction visible in its numbers. What keeps the tail a tail is the sample bill, and the sample bill is regime-dependent: heavy where the learning signal is dense, as in pretraining, thin where the signal is a scalar reward, which is the regime frontier compute is shifting into as post-training and reasoning take a growing share of budgets. The early evidence runs that way, ES fine-tuning outperforming its RL baselines at billion-parameter scale, forward-pass-only methods reaching 66B on inference-class memory, multi-site reinforcement learning already demonstrated at 32B. None of that is pretraining and none of it is frontier scale, so I still cannot size the tail. What I can say is the direction: the share of compute suited to these methods is growing, and if that continues the premium's anchor narrows to pretraining alone. Nothing here recommends buying or selling anything.

Caveats

The rental spread reflects marketplace composition, who lists, what commitments they carry, regional mix, so it is a proxy tracked consistently under a published filter rather than a clean price, and the bandwidth exhibit assumes stated precisions, step times and synchronisation schemes, where different choices shift the lines by constant factors without changing the ordering of the three families or the existence of the wall.

What Would Kill This

1. The premium series compresses below 5 percentage points and stays there for two consecutive quarters, absent a supply-side explanation such as a cluster capacity glut.

2. A documented run of at least 100B parameters completes over links of 10 Gb/s or less within 1.3 times the compute of a matched co-located baseline. Both numbers fixed here, before the fact.

3. Published, well-documented communication-reduction factors reach 5,000 times synchronous data parallelism at 10B parameters or larger: one order of magnitude beyond today's 400 to 500 times.
